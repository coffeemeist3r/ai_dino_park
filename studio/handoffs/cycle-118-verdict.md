## Lore track

**Verdict:** APPROVED
**Item:** BACKLOG-471 — The grumble reaches the keeper

**Rationale.** All 7 acceptance criteria PASS, build clean, 1423 unit and 409 e2e green on a full parallel
run, boundary grep empty, no save-shape change. The judgement worth recording is in `heldShort`: the
question "did the reserve refuse this mouth" could have been answered a dozen cheap ways — a pile total, a
count of stocked ids, a re-derived reserve constant — and every one of them would have been a *second*
definition of the reserve, free to drift from the first. It instead asks `pickFoodToSpend` twice, once with
the policy's reserve and once with zero, which makes the detection definitionally the negative space of the
spend decision it explains. A unit test pins that agreement across a matrix of piles, including the case
that catches the lazy version: a pantry whose only stocked id sits *at* the reserve reads as held-short,
where a pile-total comparison would call it empty and stay silent.

Two more calls I'd defend on review. The counters are **not persisted** — reload and the ledger is empty —
and that is right rather than lazy: the grumble reports a live standoff between a live policy and a live
pantry, and a discontent restored from a save could outlive both. And the reset lives on the *successful
spend*, not on a timer, so the grievance ends the instant the ground feeds someone. That is what keeps this
a care signal instead of a mood: it is answerable, and the keeper's food drop is the answer.

No regressions in the diff. The 463 spend behaviour is observed, never altered — `feedFromStores` decides
exactly what it decided yesterday, and the new code runs entirely inside the branch where nothing happened.

## Structure track

**Verdict:** APPROVED
**Item:** BACKLOG-465 — Per-crop seasonal yield

**Rationale.** All 9 acceptance criteria PASS on the same green run. Off-milestone, justified in the
structure handoff and accepted: M9's structure track closed at 468, so there was no arc to serve, and
finishing a spine the studio explicitly split in half — 461's own source comment names 465 as its deferred
remainder — outranks opening a fourth new one.

What makes this a good structural beat rather than a flavour knob is what it does to the systems it does
not touch. The ferry (447), the demand read (438), the migration bias (450) and the decline read (460) all
consume *differences between grounds*, and for 465 cycles the only thing that produced those differences
was chance. Now the calendar produces them, on a rotation, four times a year: summer favours the bowl and
starves the Fernreach, fall favours the Grove and starves the bowl, winter favours the Fernreach and
starves the Grove. Not one of those four systems needed a line changed — that is the read on whether the
chain economy was actually general, and it was.

Three implementation calls I'd single out. **Spring appears in no row**, so the hinge falls out of the
table's shape rather than being special-cased — and a fresh boot is byte-identical, the fourth cycle
running (171/178/461 before it) to honour that discipline. The rotation is pinned by a *shape invariant*
("each of summer/fall/winter has exactly one crop at 2 and exactly one at 0") rather than three literals,
so a fifth crop added carelessly fails a test instead of quietly flattening the year. And both ticker lines
are **generated by scanning the table**, so the sentence the player reads cannot drift from the data the
sim runs on — the same class of defect the 468 verdict flagged for glyph/type pairs, pre-empted here.

**Durability note carried forward from QA (not a defect).** Every existing harvest-driving spec plants on
day 1 and re-plants on each harvest day, so a three-harvest drive lands on days 3, 5 and 7 — the last of
those is the *final day of spring*, one in-game day short of the summer boundary where a bowl harvest
begins banking double and crediting two haulers. QA audited all thirteen such specs by hand and none chains
a fourth. The suite is green and the margin is real but unbreached; I am logging it here rather than
opening a BACKLOG item because the fix is a one-line `__setClock` pin in whichever spec first needs a
fourth harvest, and the failure would be loud (an exact-total assert), not silent.
