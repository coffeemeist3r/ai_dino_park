# Cycle 121 — Lore Handoff

**Theme:** The last arc of Milestone 10, and the one that turns a *place* into a *feeling*. This park now
has four grounds, a record of who set foot on each one first (343), and a teller who keeps the pride of
having been somewhere a friend hasn't (364). What it still doesn't have is a dino that **misses** anywhere.
Migration has always been a push — the pantry ran dry, the ground hollowed, the neighbour is richer (450 /
457 / 460). Nothing in a hundred and twenty cycles has ever been a *pull*: a dino leaving good ground for
worse because some other ground got under its skin. Tonight a place a dino has stood in and been away from
a long while starts calling it back.

knew first, cycle 120) shipped; the structure track closed cycle 120. **This is the last open arc in the
milestone.** It ships, and M10 ships with it.

**Suggested next-up:** **BACKLOG-362** — *a ground you come to miss*. Queued in cycle-79's block as the
grove-specific version ("Grove-struck homesickness — a dino back from the grove a long while files a faint
'miss the pond' pull"), which was the honest framing when the grove was the only elsewhere. The milestone
generalizes it the way 343 and 364 were generalized: **any** ground a dino has visited and has been away
from long enough starts to be missed, named in the dino's own voice, and the yearning re-primes its
migration decision toward that ground. The zone-visit record (339 / 359) already knows where every dino has
been and 364 just taught the park to read that record as standing rather than as a bit; this reads the same
record as *time since*. Deathless, deterministic floor, temperament-shaded colour on top.

**Why it's a pull and not another push:** every existing migration bias is scarcity (450), hollowing (460),
or a friend (340). Those all say *leave here*. 362 is the first that says *go there* — and the sharpest read
in it is the dino that leaves a well-fed ground anyway, because it has been away from the Hollow too long.
That is the beat that makes four grounds feel like a place a dino lives in rather than a graph it is routed
across.
